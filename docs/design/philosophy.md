# Design Philosophy

Prometheas.com is built on a design philosophy called **Synthesis** -- the deliberate fusion of Japanese minimalism and classical Greek proportion into a single, coherent visual language. Every design decision on this site traces back to one of these two traditions, and the tension between them is what gives the site its character.

This document defines the principles that govern how the site looks, feels, and breathes.

---

## Synthesis

Synthesis is not a compromise between two aesthetics. It is an integration. Japanese minimalism provides the emotional register -- quietude, restraint, the eloquence of empty space. Classical Greek design provides the structural skeleton -- column rhythm, proportional hierarchy, the logic of measurement. Together they produce interfaces that feel both serene and precisely engineered.

The name "Prometheas" itself bridges cultures: a Greek mythological figure rendered through a modern, personal lens. The design follows the same arc.

---

## Japanese Minimalism

The Japanese aesthetic tradition contributes three core principles to this design system:

**Ma (間) -- Negative Space as Content.** In Japanese art and architecture, the space between objects is not empty; it is active. Ma is the pause in a conversation that gives weight to the next word. On this site, generous whitespace is not wasted space -- it is structural. Content areas breathe. Margins are wide. Elements never crowd one another.

**Restraint.** Every element must earn its place. If a border can do the work of a shadow, use the border. If whitespace can do the work of a border, use whitespace. Ornamentation is not forbidden, but it must be purposeful. The red accent dot in the logo is a prime example: a single point of color that carries the entire brand.

**Asymmetric Balance.** Symmetry is not the only path to visual equilibrium. Japanese composition often achieves balance through the careful placement of unequal elements -- a heavy block of text offset by open space, a navigation element anchored to one side while content flows on the other.

---

## Classical Greek Proportion

The Greek tradition contributes structural rigor:

**Column Rhythm.** Content is organized along a consistent vertical grid, echoing the colonnades of classical architecture. This rhythm creates predictability and trust. The eye knows where to look next.

**Structured Hierarchy.** Typographic scale follows a deliberate progression. Headings, subheadings, body text, and captions each occupy a distinct tier in the visual hierarchy, with clear ratios between them. Nothing is ambiguous about what is primary and what is secondary.

**Proportion and the Golden Ratio Sensibility.** While the site does not mechanically apply phi (1.618) to every measurement, it operates with the same instinct: relationships between elements feel mathematically considered rather than arbitrary. Padding, margin, and line-height choices reflect proportional thinking.

---

## Visual Language

These principles manifest in concrete design choices:

- **Red dots as visual punctuation.** The Prometheas logo ends with a red dot -- a deliberate period that functions as a brand mark. This motif extends throughout the site as accent dots, hover states, and interactive indicators. Red is never used casually; it always punctuates.
- **Zinc as atmosphere.** The cool gray palette (zinc-50 through zinc-900) provides tonal depth without warmth. Zinc reads as serious, calm, and architectural -- like stone.
- **White as canvas.** The background is not merely white; it is the canvas on which everything else is composed. White space is the most important "color" in the system.
- **Borders, not shadows.** Hierarchy is established through hairline rules and subtle border distinctions, not drop shadows or elevation. This keeps the visual plane flat and honest -- nothing pretends to float above anything else.

---

## What This Site Is Not

Design is as much about what you reject as what you embrace. The following anti-patterns are explicitly excluded:

- **Not brutalist.** Brutalism celebrates rawness and deliberate ugliness as aesthetic statements. This site values refinement. Raw HTML energy is not the goal.
- **Not maximalist.** Every element competes for attention in maximalist design. Here, most elements recede so that content and key interactions can speak clearly.
- **Not corporate SaaS.** No gradient hero sections, no "trusted by 10,000+ companies" social proof bars, no rounded-corner card grids with drop shadows. The site belongs to a person, not a product.
- **Not "generic tech startup."** No stock photography of diverse teams high-fiving. No teal-and-purple color schemes. No "We're revolutionizing X" copy patterns. The site has a specific cultural identity, not a market positioning.
