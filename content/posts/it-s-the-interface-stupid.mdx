In the grand tradition of "It's the _xyz_, Stupid" arguments, I present here the assertion that _interface_ is a key design consideration of large and/or complex systems, if any meaningful capacity for evolution is to be realized. In fact, the larger and more complex the system, the greater its importance.

This is true whether the system in question is an organization's software infrastructure, a public utility, a national government, or the human body.

### Qualifying _Interface_

The term _interface_ is a bit overloaded, particularly within the context of technology. To the layperson, the term implicitly refers to the visual look and feel of software such as applications or websites. People may praise the simple "interface" of Flickr or bemoan how awful the "interface" of their accounting app at work is, but these discussions are more specifically considerations about each software's _user interface_.

But there are other types of _interfaces_ in the context of technology (and more, still, beyond).

On _Uncarved_, I will attempt always to qualify specific types of interfaces such as _user interfaces_ (UIs), _application programming interfaces_ (APIs), and others.

So then, what meaning shall we ascribe to _interface_?

> in·ter·face - n. /ˈin(t)ərˌfās/
>
> 1\. a surface regarded as the common boundary of two bodies, spaces, or phases.
> 2\. the facts, problems, considerations, theories, practices, etc., shared by two or more disciplines, procedures, or fields of study: the interface between chemistry and physics.
> 3\. a common boundary or interconnection between systems, equipment, concepts, or human beings.
