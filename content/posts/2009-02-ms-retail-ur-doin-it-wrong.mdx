Microsoft has recently [announced](http://www.reuters.com/article/ousiv/idUSTRE51B7K620090213) that they will be entering the retail space with their own stores. Clearly they feel a need to compete with Apple's success in their retail endeavors. But, in a seeming effort to remain depressingly consistent, they're once again stealing from the surface and ignoring the treasures below.

AppleInsider published [a piece covering the story](http://www.appleinsider.com/articles/09/02/12/microsoft_to_open_new_retail_stores_like_apple.html), and included a couple of photos taken in a private 20,000 square foot mock-up retail store constructed on Microsoft's campus.

The piece includes this wonderful photo, showing a customer that [we can only assume is a PC](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSiSIzXKMXw), pushing a shopping cart around the store:

[![](http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_wIu0X4L5mFk/SZXrADXYNaI/AAAAAAAAAAg/qx9tmM98_uc/s320/msretail-090212-2.jpg)](http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_wIu0X4L5mFk/SZXrADXYNaI/AAAAAAAAAAg/qx9tmM98_uc/s1600-h/msretail-090212-2.jpg)

We can infer immediately from this photo that the people designing this customer experience seem to think that shoppers will actually be pushing a shopping cart around the store.

Think about that. 

And even more interestingly, the store designers intend to "deliver targeted, real-time information to [the] shopping cart."

So, after Microsoft have finally decided to kill [Clippy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_Assistant), these store designers decide it's a great idea to create "Carty."

Worse still, this invokes the thought of another super lame Windows feature: _balloon tips_.

If you've ever used Windows, you know exactly what these are — that tedious series of yellow, comic-like speech balloons that pop out of the task tray, proudly announcing a number of painfully irrelevant "accomplishments," including finding a wifi signal, connecting successfully to the internet, and recognizing an attached printer or USB device.

Here's a little screenplay I wrote to illustrate the design goals for that shopper's experience:
<div class="screenplay">

Shopper enters the store, walks over to the Cart Nursery and grabs a shopping cart. The moment her hand touches the cart's pushbar, a voice greets her:

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Hi There! I'm Carty. I can help you make the most of your visit to the Microsoft Store. It's always a pleasure to meet a PC. Where do _you_ want to go today?

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> I'd like to get an XBox.

The shopper pulls the cart out and proceeds into the store.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Is this purchase for you, or for someone else?

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> It's for my brother.

Carty scans the RFID chip in the <span class="character">Shopper</span>'s Real ID card.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Is that Alex, or John?

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> Um... It's for Alex.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> OK. Please proceed to aisle 8\. While we're walking, did you know that there are now more A-grade titles for XBox than for PS3?

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> Huh?

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Your other brother John's PS3 purchase in 2007 seems to have proven imprudent.

<span class="character">Shopper</span> and <span class="character">Carty</span> turn into aisle 8.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Here are the XBox consoles. Did you know that a new shipment of the critically-acclaimed _Crushing Attack in 3D_ just arrived today? It's a perfect accompaniment to this purchase. Especially considering Alex's ROTC service, in 1994.

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> Hrm... Alex _does_ love his shoot-em-ups...

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Great. I'll be glad to help you with that. But before we go...

Carty scans the RFID chip in the **Shopper**'s bank card.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> I've noted you have an additional $6,488 in your checking account -- I can have a console delivered to his house in 5 business days. Allow, deny, cancel?

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> Um... Deny, Carty... Let's get over to the games, OK?

<span class="character">Carty:</span> OK. Please proceed to aisle 33.

Shopper pushes the cart to aisle 33, which is lined with XBox 360 games.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> WARNING: It took longer than expected for an able-bodied person to get here. YOU MAY BE AT RISK!! Would you like to get a FREE checkup from our in-store physician?

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> No, thanks. I'm fine today.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Did you know?- you can disable these notifications at the Customer Service Center, near the entrance.

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> OK, maybe next time. Where's this game?

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Ahead, to the right, on the top shelf.

Shopper locates a copy of _Crushing Attack in 3D_, and puts the game into the cart.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Did you know: we're offering a 15% savings on the _SuperBlaster 2100 Gun Controller_ with the purchase of _Crushing Attack in 3D_.

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> Not a bad idea -- where's that?

<span class="character">Carty:</span> In aisle 8.

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> 8? Weren't we just...

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Yes, but don't worry, it's in a new location.

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> Really?

<span class="character">Carty:</span> Relatively. You see, the aisles you _haven't_ been using have been collapsed and hidden away.

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> OK, never mind. I think this is fine. And he already has the _ShockShooter GSX_ anyway.

<span class="character">Carty:</span> I'm afraid the _ShockShooter GSX_ isn't certified with this title.

<span class="character">Shopper:</span> But they're both guns; shouldn't it just work?

<span class="character">Carty:</span> This isn't to say it definitely won't... It's just not officially certified, and may void your system's warranty.

Oddly, 85% of visiting customers that make it this far into the shopping experience will actually complete their visit with a purchase.
